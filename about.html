About 3D Earth

3D Earth is a browser-based geospatial visualization project. It brings a navigable globe, location tools, map context and weather information into one interface.

Why it was developed

3D Earth was developed because its developer, Kweezi Perez, has a deep love and near-obsession with tracking, maps and the perspective of seeing the Earth from above. There is a particular fascination with being the watcher: understanding what is moving, where it is, what is happening around it, and being able to see the bigger picture from above.

That obsession is the reason the project is built around a living globe rather than a conventional dashboard. The goal is to make the Earth the place from which everything can be understood: a location, a weather system, a flight, an earthquake, or a real satellite moving through orbit.

3D Earth is therefore both a technical project and a personal instrument. It turns the developer's fascination with watching the planet into a working geospatial interface where real geographic and orbital data can be explored in context.

What it can be used for

The project is intended to have practical value in Uganda and beyond. Students and researchers can use it to explore geography, coordinate systems, weather and orbital concepts. Developers and geospatial practitioners can use it as a working reference for browser-based mapping and 3D visualization.

For community organisations, educators and local teams, the combination of location, weather, map and environmental layers can provide a quick visual starting point for discussing weather conditions, geographic areas, environmental events, aviation context and disaster-awareness information. It is not an emergency-response system, but it can help people understand the spatial context around an event.

The longer-term direction is to make the platform useful for African geospatial learning and situational awareness while keeping the underlying sources and limitations visible.

Interface

The globe is rendered with Three.js. Location can come from the browser's positioning service or from coordinates selected on the map. Weather is requested for the selected coordinates, and OpenStreetMap provides the conventional map view. Satellite tracking uses live orbital element data from CelesTrak and SGP4 propagation to place real cataloged objects on the globe. A selected satellite can be inspected for its catalog identity, owner, launch information, orbital parameters, current position and propagated speed. The spacecraft silhouette is a 3D visual representation of the tracked object; it is not a claim that every satellite shares the same physical design. Additional layers may include flights, seismic activity and other feeds where available.

Limitations

3D Earth does not create the underlying geographic, weather or satellite data. Each source has its own accuracy, update cycle, licensing terms and availability. A visual layer should therefore be treated as context rather than as a definitive operational record.

Device positioning can be affected by hardware, signal conditions and browser settings. Weather forecasts are model output. Live feeds can be delayed, incomplete or temporarily unavailable.

Project scopeVisualization, geographic context and exploration. Not a navigation, emergency-response or safety-critical system.
